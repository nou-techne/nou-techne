RegenHub, LCA — Open Accounting

Financial Transparency

Monthly financial packages issued under the Habitat open accounting standard.

RegenHub operates as a Colorado Limited Cooperative Association, which means its economic life is structured around patronage — the principle that economic benefit flows to members in proportion to their contribution to the cooperative, not just their capital. These financial packages are how that principle is made legible, month by month.

Each W1 package (issued in the first week of the month for the prior month’s close) follows the Habitat open accounting standard: a chart of accounts, reporting format, and allocation methodology that can be adopted by any organization seeking to make its economic relationships transparent and replicable.

The long-term vision is REA (Resource, Event, Agent) — a transaction grammar that records economic exchanges as events transforming resource balances between agents, rather than debits and credits against categories. These documents are the current approximation: familiar financial structure that will become REA-native views as the tooling matures.

Habitat Chart of Accounts
1000–5999 series. Assets, liabilities, equity, revenue, and expenses structured for cooperative and commons-based organizations.

Resource, Event, Agent. Every economic exchange as an event. Financial statements as views projected over the event ledger, not the ledger itself.

The Open Accounting Standard

The Habitat standard is designed to be adopted, not just observed. Any cooperative or commons-based organization can fork the chart of accounts, adopt the W1 package format, and issue monthly financial packages that are legible to members and the public alike.

Each package contains four sections: a cash reconciliation (Section I), an income statement (Section II), a balance sheet (Section III), and an outstanding payments tracker (Section IV). Together they answer the questions any member or observer should be able to ask: Where did the money come from? Where did it go? What do we own and owe? What is still unresolved?

The source for this standard lives in the nou-techne repository under docs/accounting/. Contributions and forks are welcome.
