### 3.3 Withdrawal and Termination

**Withdrawal:** 14 days written notice. Capital account settled at IRC 704(b) book value. Board has discretion on timing/method of equity redemption; must redeem within 5 years.

**Suspension:** Board simple majority, up to 180 days. Member loses all participation rights during suspension.

**Termination triggers:** Dues 30+ days overdue; 3 months patronage inactivity (per class); misrepresentation; ineligibility; disruption. Board simple majority after 15-day notice + hearing.

### 3.4 Stock and Transfer Restrictions

- Unlimited shares authorized, no par value
- Non-transferable except back to the cooperative
- Any purported transfer void unless Board consents in writing
- Cooperative has first lien on all member equity

## 4. Capital Structure

### 4.1 Subchapter K Partnership Treatment

This is the most significant structural adaptation from the Meadow source. RegenHub elects Subchapter K partnership tax treatment rather than Subchapter T cooperative taxation:

| Dimension | Subchapter T (Meadow) | Subchapter K (RegenHub) |
|-----------|----------------------|------------------------|
| Tax entity | Cooperative | Partnership (Form 1065) |
| Member reporting | Qualified written notices of allocation | Schedule K-1 |
| Tax deductibility | Cooperative deducts qualified patronage dividends | Pass-through: income/loss allocated directly to members |
| Loss treatment | Board discretion; avoid annual member allocation | Losses pass through to members per patronage-weighted formula |
| Capital accounts | Equity account (cost basis) | IRC 704(b) book capital accounts + parallel tax capital accounts |
| Property contributions | Not addressed | 704(c) layer tracking; built-in gain/loss allocated to contributor |
| Revaluations | Not addressed | Board discretion at specified trigger events |

**Why Sub K?** The venture studio model requires: (1) loss pass-through to members (critical for early-stage ventures), (2) flexible allocation of different types of income/loss, (3) capital account precision for multi-class membership with differing economic interests.

### 4.2 Capital Accounts

Each member has two parallel accounts:

**Book Capital Account** (IRC 704(b) / Treas. Reg. 1.704-1(b)(2)(iv)):
- Increased by: cash/property contributions, allocated income and gain
- Decreased by: distributions, allocated losses and deductions
- Governs liquidation distributions

**Tax Capital Account:**
- Tracks member's tax basis in cooperative interest
- Used for K-1 preparation, gain/loss on disposition
- May diverge from book account due to 704(c) layers, timing differences

### 4.3 Substantial Economic Effect (704(b) Compliance)

Three requirements the allocation scheme must satisfy:

1. **Economic Effect Test:** Capital accounts maintained per regulations; liquidating distributions follow positive capital account balances; deficit restoration obligation (DRO) or qualified income offset (QIO) in place.

2. **Liquidation follows capital accounts:** Art. XIV dissolution waterfall distributes based on capital account balances.

3. **Substantiality:** Allocations must have reasonable possibility of affecting dollar amounts received, independent of tax consequences.

**Attorney review required:** Jeff Pote must confirm: (a) QIO vs. DRO election, (b) liquidation distribution language satisfies Treas. Reg. 1.704-1(b)(2)(ii)(b)(2), (c) 704(c) method selection (Traditional / Curative / Remedial).

## 6. The Venture Basket Model

### 6.1 Core Concept

Each founding venture commits 10% of its equity to RegenHub as a cooperative gift. This is not a sale — it is a structural commitment to root each venture in the ecosystem. The equity is allocated:

| Pool | Allocation | Purpose |
|------|-----------|---------|
| Basket fund | 1–2% per venture | Available to investor members |
| Balance sheet reserve | 8–9% per venture | Held by cooperative for future seasons |

> The closest analog is community-supported agriculture. Instead of produce, investors receive equity in a basket of RegenHub Ventures. The periodicity (seasonality) creates: what is emergent within a season? What is growing between seasons?

## 7. Intellectual Property Architecture

### 7.1 Works Made for Hire

The Member Agreement (§ 3.1) establishes that work within a member's scope of direct service to the cooperative is "works made for hire" — cooperative owns it.

### 7.2 Critical Carveouts

Two explicit carveouts protect founders:

1. **Applicant IP:** Previously developed IP proprietary to the member or licensed from third parties
2. **Venture IP:** IP developed in the course of operating, developing, or building a venture incubated at or affiliated with RegenHub — remains property of the applicable venture and its founders

This is the critical structural distinction that makes the venture studio model work: cooperative service IP flows to the cooperative; venture IP stays with the venture.

## 13. Analytical Observations

### 13.1 Structural Strengths

1. **Colorado LCA is purpose-built for this.** The ULCAA was specifically designed to let cooperatives accept outside investment without sacrificing democratic governance. Non-voting investor membership is a first-class statutory concept, not a workaround.

2. **Sub K election is the right call for a venture studio.** Loss pass-through, flexible allocation, and capital account precision are essential for an entity that will have widely varying contribution types and economic interests across member classes.

3. **The venture IP carveout is critical.** Without it, the cooperative's works-for-hire provision would sweep in venture IP, destroying the incentive for founders to incubate within the cooperative. This single provision makes the venture studio model structurally viable.

4. **The basket model is elegant.** By converting venture equity gifts into a diversified fund, the cooperative transforms concentrated venture risk into ecosystem exposure. New ventures added over time create compounding without dilution.

5. **Deferred return terms are strategically sound.** Defining no return at signing and deferring to the FSC preserves cooperative autonomy and prevents early capital from locking in terms that constrain future governance.

### 13.2 Areas Requiring Attention

1. **The voting rights inconsistency is the most urgent structural question.** The Feb 27 decision and the bylaws draft disagree. If Class 1 only votes, Classes 2 and 3 become economically participating but governance-excluded patron members — which creates tension with ICA Principle 2 (Democratic Member Control) since they are patron members.

2. **The patronage formula needs formal specification.** The 40/30/20/10 weights are proposed but undefined in terms of measurement methodology. How is "labor hours" counted? How is "revenue generation" attributed to individual members? How is "community building" measured? Without operational definitions, the formula is aspirational, not implementable.

3. **The Meadow → RegenHub adaptation has untested edges.** The Sub K rewrite is extensive but was done by adapting an existing Sub T document rather than drafting from scratch for Sub K. Jeff Pote's review is essential to catch any Sub T remnants or Sub K requirements that weren't fully addressed.

4. **Schedule A is entirely TBD.** No share prices or dues have been set for any class. This blocks member admission — you can't execute a Member Agreement without knowing what the member pays.
