# Article Skeleton: Bioregional Finance Is Not Tokenizing Bioregions
## A disambiguation — and a path toward encoding natural law

*Progressive structure: this skeleton expands from article → essay → whitepaper*

### I. The Confusion (500 words)

**Opening move:** Etymology of *bioregion* — Greek *bios* (life) + Latin *regio* (direction, boundary, district). A bioregion is not a resource to be represented. It is the living context within which economic relationships occur.

- The "tokenizing bioregions" imagination: bioregion as asset class
- What bioregional finance actually is: making place-based relationships programmable
- Why the confusion matters: misframing leads to extractive "green" financialization
- The Bollier warning: "$7 trillion subsidizes harm; $200 billion flows to protection"

### II. What Is Already Working (800 words)

Four systems running today that are *not* tokenizing bioregions:

**A. Community Inclusion Currencies — Grassroots Economics (Kenya)**
- Commitment pooling: mutual credit from real goods and services
- Works on feature phones, no crypto sophistication required
- 2025 survey: measurable impact for low-income families
- The currency circulates *because it represents commitments of people in that place*

**B. Verified Ecological Credits — Regen Network**
- Credits verified against actual ecological state changes
- Biocultural stewardship methodology — Indigenous integration
- Not derivatives of nature; evidence of ecological work

**C. Bioregional Textile Systems — Fibershed**
- The "shed" as organizing unit: fiber from within 150 miles
- Integrates agriculture, processing, manufacturing, culture
- Demonstrates the pattern: housing shed, food shed, water shed, fiber shed

**D. Bounty-Driven Coordination — Swarm Ecosystems**
- Tokens fund work within a bioregion, not represent the bioregion itself
- Knowledge compounds in commons repositories

### IV. The Legal Innovation: Purpose Trusts Encoding Natural Law (1,000 words)

**A. What Purpose Trusts Are**
- Trust without beneficiaries — exists to serve a *purpose*
- Enforcer appointed (not beneficiary standing)
- Voting rights in trust, economic rights allocable
- Can own land, businesses, resources — and conduct market activity

**B. The Four Sheds as Trust Purposes**
- Watershed: holding water rights for commons use
- Foodshed: agricultural land removed from speculative markets
- Fibershed: fiber-producing land, processing infrastructure, design knowledge
- Housing shed: real estate decommodified while enabling cross-subsidy

**C. "Natural Law" as Trust Purpose**
- Not metaphysical — recognition that certain resources are preconditions for life
- Purpose trust encodes this: removes resource from market alienability, binds fiduciary duty to purpose, operates in perpetuity
- The "Right to Commons" agreement as the trust's constitutional document

**D. Where This Can Happen**
- Wyoming: perpetual, no beneficiary requirement, asset protection
- Colorado tensions: prior appropriation vs. commons holding
- Emerging models: KCT (Philadelphia), Vesi (national scaling), Patagonia (business ownership)

### V. From Cooperative to Commons (500 words)

**The progression:**
1. Cooperative (LCA) — patronage accounting, democratic governance
2. Composable tools — Treasury, People, Agreements as infrastructure
3. Purpose trust — perpetual legal structure holding bioregional assets
4. Bioregional Financing Facility — trust + cooperative + tools = integrated infrastructure

**Why cooperatives are the right operating entity for purpose trusts:**
- Already practice democratic resource governance
- Patronage accounting tracks contribution and allocation transparently
- Venture reciprocity creates commons funding without extraction
- LCA structure (Colorado) allows both patron and investor membership

**The question is not "will this happen?" but "what is the minimum viable unit?"**
- One watershed? One neighborhood? One food system?
- The answer: start where you have social infrastructure, then grow the legal structure around it

### VI. Close (300 words)

Return to etymology. *Regio* — the act of drawing a boundary, of saying "this place." Bioregional finance is the act of drawing economic boundaries that correspond to ecological ones. Not to wall off, but to make visible what flows where.

The purpose trust does not create new rights. It *encodes* ones that are older than any statute — the recognition that water, food, fiber, and shelter are not commodities but conditions for life. What it adds is legal durability: a structure that outlasts any administration, any market cycle, any individual intention.

The invitation is not to wait for this to be built. It is to recognize that pieces are already running — community currencies in Kenya, eco-credits on chain, fiber systems in California, patronage accounting in Colorado. What remains is the legal architecture that holds them together across time.
