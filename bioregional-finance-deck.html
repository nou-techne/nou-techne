Bioregional Finance Is Not Tokenizing Bioregions

A disambiguation — and a path toward encoding natural law and Earth law into perpetual purpose trusts to secure the right to commons

Techne Institute / Spring 2026

Slide I

The Confusion Is Structural

When people hear "bioregional finance," many imagine tokenizing a bioregion — creating a digital asset that represents a geographic area, its ecological health, or its natural capital. This imagination treats the bioregion as an asset class to be financialized.

Bioregional finance is the opposite move. It asks: what financial instruments arise naturally from place-based relationships? The bioregion is not the asset — it is the context within which economic relationships become legible.

Tokenizing Bioregions Bioregional Finance
Orientation Bioregion as asset to represent Bioregion as context for relationship
Mechanism Creates new instruments from nature Makes existing relationships programmable
Value Flow Extracted upward to holders Circulates within the bioregion
Denomination Global markets Local commitments
Promise "Investable nature" "Relationship-grounded economy"

"The challenge is less about channeling more capital to bioregional change in the short term than in changing the fundamental dynamics of finance itself."

David Bollier & Natasha Hulst, 2026

Bollier and Hulst propose "transvestment" — the movement of money from capitalist to commons-stewarded circuits of value — and "relationalized finance" based on place-committed social relationships. The key insight is not about capital scarcity. It is about capital direction.

The problem is not that nature is not "investable." The problem is that 35 times more capital flows toward destruction than restoration, and the private sector provides less than a fifth of what does flow.

Slide II

What Is Already Working

The near-term skepticism about bioregional finance misses what is already running. Four systems demonstrate different facets of the same idea — and none of them "tokenize a bioregion."

Community Currencies

Sarafu Network
Kenya, expanding globally

Community Inclusion Currencies operating as commitment pools. Groups create mutual credit based on real goods and services they provide. Works on feature phones via USSD.

The currency circulates because it represents commitments of people in that place. No bioregion was tokenized.

Verified Eco-Credits

Regen Network
Global registry

Credits verified against actual ecological state changes — soil carbon, biodiversity, water quality. Biocultural Stewardship Methodology integrates Indigenous land stewardship.

Not derivatives of nature. Evidence of ecological work performed in a specific place.

Bioregional Textiles

Fibershed
Northern California, expanding

Regional fiber systems integrating agriculture, processing, manufacturing, and culture within a 150-mile radius. Fiber byproducts feed food and building material systems.

Demonstrates the "shed" as organizing unit — the same pattern applies to food, water, and housing.

Bounty Coordination

Swarm Ecosystems
Distributed, bioregion-linked

Agents — human and AI — claim bounties for ecological coordination tasks. Knowledge compounds in commons repositories. Mechanisms evolve based on observed outcomes.

Tokens fund work within a bioregion, not represent the bioregion itself. The distinction matters.

Each of these systems shares a common grammar: value is created and circulated within a place-based community, verified against actual conditions in that place, and governed by the people who live there. No abstract derivative is required.

Slide III

Bioregional Financing Facilities

The 2024 book Bioregional Financing Facilities: Reimagining Finance to Regenerate Our Planet (Dark Matter Labs, Buckminster Fuller Institute) proposes BFFs as a new layer in the global financial architecture — not another intermediary, but a structural change in who makes allocation decisions.

The Three-Pillar Architecture
Pillar I
Bioregional Foundation
Holds assets in perpetuity. Manages systemic investing funds. Governed by bioregional community.
Stewardship + Governance
Pillar II
Investment Company
Attracts return-seeking capital while prioritizing holistic returns beyond financial profit.
Capital Allocation
Pillar III
Bioregional Bank
Low-interest loans, technical assistance. May issue complementary currencies backed by ecosystem health.
Circulation + Credit
"There is significant risk that if these resources flow through the existing financial architecture, they could lead to further commodification, privatisation, financialisation, and centralisation of natural assets and wealth." BFFs, Dark Matter Labs / BFI, 2024

This is the exact risk that "tokenizing bioregions" represents. The BFF framework explicitly addresses it by designing allocation mechanisms that keep decision-making local and accountable. The connective tissue between financial resources and on-the-ground regenerators is governed from the ground up, not administered from above.

The Design Constraint

Closing the "nature finance gap" alone is not sufficient. Where resources are spent, how financing is structured, and who gets to make those decisions is as important as the quantum of capital. The BFF template makes this constraint architectural, not aspirational.

Slide IV

Purpose Trust Architecture

A purpose trust differs from an ordinary trust in one fundamental way: it has no ascertainable beneficiaries. It exists to serve a purpose. An enforcer is appointed to hold the trustee accountable — not to any person's wealth, but to the purpose itself.

This makes purpose trusts uniquely suited to holding commons. The trust does not serve shareholders, donors, or members. It serves the stated purpose — in perpetuity.

U.S. Legal Landscape

Jurisdiction Duration Key Features
Wyoming Strongest Perpetual Most expansive non-charitable purpose trust statute. No beneficiary requirement. Strong asset protection. No state income tax.
South Dakota Perpetual No state income tax. Dynasty trust traditions. Established trust industry.
Nevada 365 years Strong asset protection. Some restrictions on non-charitable trusts.
Florida Perpetual Recent expansion of purpose trust law. Growing jurisdiction.
Delaware 21 years Most established trust jurisdiction overall, but short non-charitable duration is limiting for perpetual commons.

Precedents in Practice

Patagonia (2022) — Yvon Chouinard transferred ownership to the Patagonia Purpose Trust (voting rights) and Holdfast Collective (economic rights directed to environmental causes). Demonstrates the voting/economic split that makes purpose trusts powerful for mission alignment.

Kensington Corridor Trust (2019) — Takes real estate in North Philadelphia off the market and places it in perpetual purpose trust for collective community control. Board includes neighborhood residents and business owners. Demonstrates the decommodification function: real estate can never be sold to the highest bidder.

Vesi — Startup deploying KCT-like model nationally. Goal: acquire pilot portfolios of 30-40 properties per neighborhood, scale while maintaining affordability and occupant equity.

Why Not a Nonprofit or CLT?

Purpose trusts can engage in market-rate transactions and use revenue to cross-subsidize mission-aligned activities. This makes them financially self-sustaining in ways 501(c)(3)s and community land trusts typically are not. A purpose trust can own businesses, conduct commerce, and generate revenue — all while bound by fiduciary duty to its stated purpose rather than to shareholder returns.

Slide V

The Four Sheds

A "shed" is a geographic area defined by a specific resource flow — the most familiar being the watershed. The same organizing principle applies to food, fiber, and housing. Each shed is a natural unit of commons governance: the boundary is drawn by ecology, not administration.

Four Sheds as Trust Purposes
∇
Watershed
aqua — water
The geographic area draining to a common point. The most legally established shed concept. Water rights, instream flows, aquifer recharge.
Precedent: Colorado Water Conservation Board holds instream flow rights. Community ditch companies govern allocation cooperatively.
≡
Foodshed
panis — bread
The geographic area providing food to a population. Agricultural land, processing, distribution, markets. Removes farmland from speculative markets.
Precedent: Farmers Land Trust — farmland for diversified production of food, forage, fiber, medicine, and ecological stewardship.
∼
Fibershed
filum — thread
The geographic landscape providing resources for locally produced textiles and building materials. Integrates agriculture, processing, and design.
Precedent: Rebecca Burgess, Fibershed — 150-mile wardrobe challenge became a regional fiber system that builds soil.
□
Housing Shed
domus — home
The geographic area within which people live and work. Real estate decommodified while enabling market-rate activity to cross-subsidize affordability.
Precedent: Kensington Corridor Trust — perpetual purpose trust holding real estate for community benefit in Philadelphia.

A purpose trust encoding bioregional rights does not secure each shed independently. The sheds overlap — the watershed feeds the foodshed, the foodshed produces fiber, housing clusters where food and water are accessible. The trust holds the relationships between sheds as much as the individual resources.

Slide VI

Encoding Natural Law and Earth Law

"Natural law" in this context is not metaphysical. It is the recognition that certain resources are preconditions for life and therefore cannot legitimately be treated as purely private property. "Earth law" provides the legal theory: ecosystems have inherent rights independent of human utility.

The purpose trust is the encoding mechanism. It translates these recognitions into durable legal structure.

How the Encoding Works
Natural Law
Recognition
Water, food, fiber, and shelter are preconditions for life — not commodities. Access to them is a right older than any statute.
Earth Law
Legal Theory
Ecosystems have inherent rights. Procedural environmental rights to establish, maintain, participate in, and seek redress for ecological commons. (Wijdekop / Earth Law Alliance)
Purpose Trust
Legal Structure
Removes resource from market alienability. Binds fiduciary duty to the purpose. Appoints enforcers with standing. Operates in perpetuity.
Right to Commons
Constitutional Document
The trust instrument declares that access to the four sheds within a bioregion is a right. The trust exists to secure that right across generations.

The Public Trust Doctrine Connection

The Public Trust Doctrine in U.S. common law already requires states to manage certain natural resources for public benefit — primarily navigable waters and submerged lands. Recent lawsuits seek expansion to atmosphere, groundwater, and broader ecosystems.

A purpose trust can serve as a private implementation of public trust principles — encoding the same recognition (that certain resources belong to the commons) in a structure that does not depend on state action or legislative will. Where the state fails its public trust obligations, a purpose trust provides an alternative enforcement path.

Colorado Tensions

Colorado's prior appropriation doctrine — "first in time, first in right" — is constitutionally embedded and fundamentally privatizes water through use-based claims. Water rights must be put to "beneficial use" or they are abandoned. This creates direct tension with commons holding.

Yet Colorado also recognizes that environmental use can constitute "beneficial use" (the Water Conservation Board holds instream flow rights), and community ditch companies — quasi-cooperative governance structures with centuries of precedent — demonstrate that water governance need not be purely individualistic. A purpose trust holding water rights for distributed community use would require novel legal argument, but the precedents exist.

Why Cooperatives Are the Right Operating Entity

A purpose trust holds assets. But assets need management — active stewardship, maintenance, allocation of revenue, response to changing conditions. The cooperative provides the operating layer:

Democratic governance — members govern the cooperative that manages the trust's assets. Decisions about resource allocation flow from the people who live in the bioregion.

Patronage accounting — tracks contribution and distributes surplus based on participation, not ownership. Every member has a transparent relationship with the cooperative's capital.

Venture reciprocity — the 1% commitment from ventures creates commons funding without extraction. Capital flows back to the trust, not out to external shareholders.

Legal compatibility — Colorado's LCA statute allows both patron and investor membership. The cooperative can raise capital from investors while maintaining patron governance — the same voting/economic split that makes purpose trusts powerful.

"The question is not will this happen, but what is the minimum viable unit? One watershed? One neighborhood? One food system?"

The answer: start where you have social infrastructure

The legal structure grows around the social infrastructure, not the other way around. Where people already cooperate — in a ditch company, a food co-op, a neighborhood association, a fiber network — the purpose trust formalizes what is already happening and makes it durable across generations.

Slide VIII

What Remains to Be Resolved

This article is the first stage of a progressive publication: article, then extended essay, then whitepaper with legal analysis. The following questions define the research frontier.

  1. Can a Wyoming perpetual purpose trust hold Colorado water rights? Interstate trust/property law intersection. The trust must comply with Colorado water law regardless of where it is domiciled. Novel question with no direct precedent.
  2. How does the "beneficial use" requirement interact with commons holding? Instream flow rights are one model. Holding water for distributed community use may need novel legal arguments beyond existing environmental beneficial use categories.
  3. What is the tax treatment of a purpose trust holding bioregional assets? Non-charitable purpose trusts are taxed differently from charitable trusts and nonprofits. The Harrison (2024) Tax Notes analysis is the starting point for this research.
  4. How does the Public Trust Doctrine intersect with purpose trust law? PTD is state common law; purpose trusts are statutory. Could a purpose trust serve as a private implementation of public trust principles?
  5. What role do Indigenous sovereignty and treaty rights play? Any Front Range bioregional trust must engage with Arapaho, Cheyenne, and Ute historical claims. The BioFi Project explicitly partners with Indigenous communities. This is not optional.
  6. How does the LCA cooperative structure compose with a purpose trust? Could a cooperative be the operating entity of a purpose trust? The LCA manages economic activity; the trust holds assets in perpetuity. The legal composition is unexplored.
  7. What is the minimum viable "shed"? A full bioregional financing facility is a long-term vision. The smallest functional unit might be a single watershed, a single neighborhood, or a single food system. Starting small and growing the legal structure around existing social infrastructure.
