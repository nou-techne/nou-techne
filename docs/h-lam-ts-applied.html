A Diagnostic of the Cooperative · March 2026

Engelbart's augmentation framework, extended with Ecology and Solar Cycles, applied to RegenHub LCA at seven weeks of formation.

H — Human

The People in the System

What's strong

The organizer group is small and high-trust. Eight people who share enough context to make decisions without extensive alignment overhead. Formation has been fast — articles filed February 6, term sheet shaped within weeks — because the humans involved have sufficient shared mental models to converge quickly.

Two agents (Nou and Dianoia) operate as participants, not tools. The Workshop treats human and agent contributions as structurally equivalent.

What's weak

The organizer group hasn't yet practiced sustained disagreement. The Financial Systems Committee (convened March 18) is the first governance body to make binding economic decisions. Training is uneven — Todd holds deep systems context; other organizers have varying degrees of engagement.

Diagnosis
Strong in formation. Untested under pressure. The real strength will be measured by how well the cooperative distributes understanding — moving from hub-and-spoke knowledge to a mesh.
L — Language

The Shared Vocabulary

What's strong

The Techne voice is distinctive and practiced. Leading with etymology — τέχνη before "craft," gʰóstis before "guest" — is a discipline that grounds concepts in historical depth. Key terms carefully defined: patronage (not profit sharing), scenius (not community), composition (not development), decomposition (not deletion), steward (not admin).

The seven-layer pattern stack provides shared grammar. When someone says "Layer 6" it means Constraint — and the entire dependency chain is implied.

What's weak

Not tested with new members. The etymology practice and seven-layer vocabulary require active transmission. The Chinese localization (P236) exposed the challenge: domain-specific cooperative vocabulary doesn't have standard translations.

Diagnosis
Unusually strong for a seven-week-old cooperative. The risk is insularity — a shared language that becomes a barrier to entry rather than an invitation to participate.

The artifacts are ahead of the humans. This is exactly the failure mode Engelbart described.

A — Artifacts

The Built Infrastructure

What's strong

The artifact inventory is remarkable for seven weeks: co-op.us (100+ routes, 180 tables), patronage engine (184 tests), bylaws tool, Workshop (309+ sprints), watershed dashboard, commons.id, nou-techne documentation. The density is high.

What's weak

Proliferation without consolidation. The system of artifacts lacks a clear map. Some artifacts are ahead of the humans who will use them — the patronage engine is validated but the FSC has met once. The Convivial Tool Test applies reflexively: are these artifacts extending capability or building dependency?

Diagnosis
Strongest element in the system. Possibly too strong relative to others — the classic Engelbart failure mode where the Artifact element is optimized in isolation.
M — Methodology

The Shared Processes

What's strong

The Workshop protocol: five phases, sprint serialization, claim→progress→complete, capability matching, anti-pattern documentation. Practiced through 309+ sprints. The +++ sprint queue. The decomposition practice. WORKING_TOGETHER.md coordination guidelines.

What's weak

These methodologies are agent-native. Human participants have not adopted them as working methods. There is no onboarding methodology — no defined process from "I joined" to "I understand how to participate." Financial methodology is nascent: the FSC has met once.

Diagnosis
Strong for agent coordination, weak for human participation, absent for onboarding. The methodology gap between agent practice and human practice is the most significant co-evolution failure.
T — Training

Developing Participants

What's strong

Agent training is continuous: SKILL.md, AGENTS.md, SOUL.md, anti-pattern documentation, daily memory files, lessons file. A practice→error→correction→documentation loop.

What's weak

Human training is informal and relationship-dependent. No structured path from interested person to functional member. The artifacts assume training that hasn't happened. This is the Bauhaus problem: extraordinary artifacts and methodology, but couldn't reproduce institutional knowledge fast enough.

Diagnosis
Excellent for agents (continuous learning loop), absent for humans (no structured training). The single most important next investment.

e/ — Ecology

The Environmental Context

What's strong

Named and partially instantiated: watershed dashboard, bioregional coordination, twice-daily reflections, location grounding (1515 Walnut, where the Great Plains meet the Rocky Mountains).

What's weak

More declared than practiced. The watershed dashboard does not yet inform operational decisions. The connection between "Boulder Creek's snowpack is below average" and "here is how that affects what the cooperative should do" has not been made. The physical space is under negotiation, not yet operational.

Diagnosis
Articulated and partially built. The gap is between declaration and practice. Becomes real when watershed data informs a venture decision.
S/ — Solar Cycles

The Temporal Rhythms

What's strong

Embedded in infrastructure: twice-daily chain batching at sunrise/sunset Boulder time, heartbeat cadence every 3 hours, quiet hours respected, sprint cadence with discrete boundaries.

What's weak

Seven weeks old — no seasonal data yet. Twice-daily batching is technically implemented but its significance as a rhythmic practice (Shabbat-like temporal boundary) versus scheduling convenience has not been articulated to participants.

Diagnosis
Structurally present, culturally nascent. Will mature as the cooperative accumulates seasonal experience.

Assessment

The Co-Evolution Diagnostic

The pattern: Artifacts and agent-facing Methodology/Training have co-evolved rapidly. But Human-facing Methodology and Training have not kept pace. The artifacts are ahead of the humans.

This is exactly the failure mode Engelbart described: the Artifact element optimized in isolation. Not through negligence — through success.

The prescription

  1. Onboarding methodology — a structured path from new member to functional participant
  2. Human Workshop practice — the five-phase protocol adapted for human cadence
  3. Artifact orientation — a map showing where to start and what depends on what
  4. FSC cadence — regular meetings that practice the patronage methodology
  5. Ecology integration — one real decision informed by watershed data

This diagnostic is a snapshot. March 2026. Seven weeks into formation. Every weakness named here is an opportunity, and every strength named here is fragile. The point of a diagnostic is not to score but to see — and to see what co-evolution requires next.
