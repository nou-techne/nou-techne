## Issue 1: Intellectual Property — Scope Misaligned with Cooperative Model

### Current language (Section 3.3)

> "the Cooperative shall have title to, ownership of, and all proprietary rights in and to any Intellectual Property, including all works-in-progress, developed or created within Applicant's scope of their service (if any) to the Cooperative… all of which shall be considered 'works made for hire'"

The portfolio license-back (Section 3.3.3) requires prior written permission from the Cooperative on each occasion before a member may display their own work in their portfolio.

### Problem

"Scope of their service" is undefined and overbroad. Patron members are owner-practitioners, not employees or contractors. A member who writes software, designs a system, produces a curriculum, or creates visual work while participating in Techne cooperative activities could inadvertently assign that IP entirely to the Cooperative — with a license-back more restrictive than most employment agreements.

This is not required by Colorado LCA statute. C.R.S. § 7-58-602 defines patron member interests as financial rights and governance rights tied to patronage; IP assignment is purely contractual and should reflect the cooperative's actual economic model. For a cooperative of builders and practitioners, the current provision is backwards.

### Proposed modifications

**Add the following definition:**

> **"Cooperative-Commissioned Work"** means Intellectual Property developed by Applicant pursuant to a written task assignment, statement of work, or engagement letter executed by an authorized officer of the Cooperative, expressly identifying the deliverable as a work-made-for-hire for the Cooperative, and for which Applicant has received or is entitled to receive compensation from the Cooperative beyond the standard member share and any patronage allocation.

**Replace Section 3.3 works-made-for-hire scope** to read:

> The Cooperative shall have title to, ownership of, and all proprietary rights in and to any Intellectual Property constituting Cooperative-Commissioned Work. All such Cooperative-Commissioned Work shall be considered "works made for hire" as defined by 17 U.S.C. §§ 101 and 201(b). To the extent any Cooperative-Commissioned Work is not deemed a work made for hire, Applicant hereby assigns all right, title, and interest therein to the Cooperative. Intellectual Property developed by Applicant outside the scope of Cooperative-Commissioned Work — including Applicant's independent professional practice, open-source contributions, and prior work — remains Applicant's property and is not affected by this Agreement.

**Replace Section 3.3.3 (portfolio license):**

> The Cooperative grants Applicant a perpetual, royalty-free, non-exclusive license to display Cooperative-Commissioned Work in Applicant's professional portfolio and in the marketing of Applicant's services. Before any such display, Applicant shall provide the Cooperative written notice identifying the specific work and proposed use. The Cooperative may, within ten (10) business days of such notice, provide written objection based solely on a specific client confidentiality obligation binding on the Cooperative. Absent a timely objection, Applicant may proceed.

### Drafting note for Jeff

Confirm whether the Cooperative has any existing client contracts that require broader IP control over member deliverables. If so, those constraints should flow down through individual engagement letters, not through the base membership agreement.

## Issue 2: Voluntary Withdrawal and Membership Interest Redemption

### Current gap

The agreement addresses termination (Cooperative expels member) but is silent on voluntary resignation. Section 9.1 prohibits transfer without written consent but provides no exit mechanism or redemption process for the $100 Class A stock.

### Statutory basis

- **C.R.S. § 7-58-1101(1):** "A member has the power to dissociate at any time, rightfully or wrongfully, by notice in a record." This is a statutory right; the agreement cannot eliminate it, only provide the implementing mechanics.
- **C.R.S. § 7-58-1006:** Redemption or repurchase of distributed property requires board authorization and is governed by the articles or bylaws. Board has discretion to deny.
- **C.R.S. § 7-58-1007:** Distributions (including redemptions) are limited when the association is insolvent or the distribution would render it insolvent.

The statute gives patron members a statutory right to dissociate; the agreement should implement the mechanics.

### Proposed addition — new Section 5.6 (Voluntary Withdrawal)

> **Voluntary Withdrawal.** Applicant may voluntarily withdraw from membership at any time by providing written notice to the Cooperative. Withdrawal is effective upon the date the Cooperative receives such notice, unless Applicant specifies a later effective date. Upon the effective withdrawal date: (a) Applicant's right to access Cooperative services, participate in governance, and receive future profit allocations shall terminate; and (b) the Cooperative shall redeem Applicant's Class A stock at its original purchase price ($100.00 per share) within ninety (90) days of the effective date, subject to (i) the limitations on distributions under C.R.S. § 7-58-1007, (ii) any outstanding financial obligations of Applicant to the Cooperative as of the withdrawal date, and (iii) Board authorization as required by C.R.S. § 7-58-1006. Any patronage credits formally noticed and allocated to Applicant's capital account but not yet distributed shall be redeemed in accordance with the Cooperative's then-current patronage redemption schedule. Applicant's obligations under Sections 3.3 (Intellectual Property), 4 (Confidentiality), 6 (Limitation of Liability), 7 (Indemnification), and 9 (General Provisions) survive withdrawal.

### Question for Jeff

Is $100 par the intended redemption price on voluntary withdrawal, or does the board want discretion to adjust for changes in book value? The answer affects whether the 90-day / par-value language is right, or whether the bylaws should govern redemption price separately.

## Issue 3: Patronage Accounting and Federal Tax Alignment

### Current language (Section 2.3)

> "The Board establishes the formula for profit share and may change the formula at the Board's discretion."

### Problem — two layers

**Layer 1 — IRC § 704(b) (Subchapter K / partnership treatment).** If the Cooperative is treated as a partnership for federal tax purposes, allocations must have "substantial economic effect" under Treas. Reg. § 1.704-1(b)(2). The safe harbor requires: (a) capital accounts maintained per regulation standards; (b) liquidating distributions made in accordance with positive capital account balances; and (c) a deficit restoration obligation or qualified income offset. Full Board discretion with no documented capital account framework undermines this safe harbor.

**Layer 2 — IRC §§ 1381–1388 (Subchapter T / cooperative treatment).** If the Cooperative pursues cooperative/patronage tax treatment, qualified patronage dividends (IRC § 1382(b)) require written notices of allocation to members specifying the member's patronage allocation. These are required at the time of payment or allocation, not retroactively.

**Statutory basis — Colorado.** C.R.S. § 7-58-1004 sets default allocation rules: absent bylaws specification, all profits must be allocated to patron members in proportion to their patronage. The Board may set aside reserves before allocation but the proportionality principle is the statutory default.

### Proposed additions

**New Section 2.4 (Patronage Plan):**

> **Patronage Plan.** The Board shall adopt a written Patronage Plan within ninety (90) days of admitting the first patron member. The Patronage Plan shall specify: (a) the method for calculating each patron member's patronage for each fiscal year; (b) the basis for allocating net margins among patron members in proportion to their patronage, consistent with C.R.S. § 7-58-1004; (c) the form and timing of patronage dividends, including the minimum percentage to be paid in cash; (d) the procedures for issuing written notices of allocation to each patron member; and (e) the procedures for maintaining and adjusting patron capital accounts. The Board may amend the Patronage Plan, provided that any amendment reducing the cash component of patronage dividends or changing the patronage basis shall require approval by a majority of patron members at a duly noticed meeting.

**Add to Section 3.3 (Consent to Tax Treatment):**

> Applicant further acknowledges that: (a) the Cooperative intends to maintain capital accounts for patron members in accordance with Treasury Regulation § 1.704-1(b)(2)(iv); (b) the Cooperative will provide Applicant with annual written notices of allocation reflecting Applicant's distributive share of net income or loss for each fiscal year; and (c) such allocations are intended to have substantial economic effect within the meaning of IRC § 704(b).

### Question for Jeff

Does RegenHub intend to be taxed as a partnership (Subchapter K) or as a cooperative (Subchapter T)? These regimes have different documentation requirements. Subchapter T requires written notices of allocation for qualified patronage dividends; Subchapter K requires capital account maintenance. It may be possible to pursue both, but the choice affects how the Patronage Plan is structured and what the annual notice process looks like. Todd has referenced a tax lawyer ("her") who should be brought into this conversation before the board adopts the Patronage Plan.

## Issue 4: Recital — Foundational Framing

### Current language

> "RegenHub is a coworking space and community venue in Boulder, Colorado, providing affordable workspace and event infrastructure for technologies, creatives, and changemakers building towards regenerative futures."

### Problem

This describes a real-estate and facilities operation. It does not reflect:
- The Cooperative's stated public benefit: "Cultivating scenius"
- Its Colorado Public Benefit LCA status
- Its hybrid membership base (Boulder-based + virtual)
- Its orientation as a cooperative of practice, not a facility operator

Foundational framing in recitals affects interpretive disputes over the entire agreement. A member's obligations under confidentiality, IP assignment, and service scope will be read against this backdrop. The current framing misrepresents the actual entity.

### Proposed replacement recital

> RegenHub, LCA ("Cooperative") is a Colorado Public Benefit Limited Cooperative Association organized to cultivate collective intelligence and scenius — the intelligence that arises from communities of practice — through right relationship between tools and the people who use them. The Cooperative serves patron members who are practitioners, builders, and thinkers operating across Boulder, Colorado and aligned virtual and regional communities. The Cooperative may operate physical gathering space, digital infrastructure, programs, and services in furtherance of this public benefit purpose.

## Issue 5: Termination — Procedural Protections for Patron Members

### Current structure

The Board may terminate membership for: failure to comply with governing documents; non-payment (30 days); false statement; conduct "detrimental to operations/reputation"; "good faith belief" of inaccurate reporting; conduct inhibiting other members' participation. Only non-payment includes a cure period.

### Problem

Patron members are owner-members of the Cooperative, not at-will participants. Termination without notice, opportunity to respond, or appeal is procedurally thin for an owner class. Colorado's ULCAA (C.R.S. § 7-58-1101) permits associations to specify termination events in governing documents, which gives the board latitude — but the membership agreement should provide minimum procedural protections aligned with cooperative governance principles.

### Proposed modification — add after Section 5's list of grounds

> **Notice and Cure.** Before exercising any termination right under this Section 5 (other than under Section 5(b) [non-payment] or Section 5(c) [false statement], which may proceed directly after the specified period or occurrence), the Cooperative shall provide Applicant with: (a) written notice specifying the ground(s) for the proposed termination and identifying the conduct or omission at issue; and (b) a thirty (30)-day period to cure, where the breach is reasonably capable of cure. Termination shall be carried out in accordance with the dispute resolution procedures in the Bylaws. Prior to any final termination determination, Applicant shall have the right to submit a written response to the Board or its designated committee.

### Note on bylaws alignment

This provision only works if the bylaws contain a real dispute resolution procedure. Before authorizing this agreement, the board should confirm that the bylaws' dispute resolution provision provides for member notice and response.

## Issue 6: Membership Class Architecture — Explicit Scope Statement

### Gap

The agreement does not explicitly identify itself as the Class A Patron Member agreement. Given that a separate Techne co-op agreement is anticipated, both agreements need clear scope statements to avoid ambiguity about which governs which class.

### Proposed addition — Section 1, opening sentence

> This Agreement governs Class A patron membership in RegenHub, LCA. It does not govern investor membership or any other membership class established by the Board after the date of this Agreement.

### Proposed addition — Section 1.1, after stock price mechanics

> The Board may establish additional membership classes with different financial rights, governance rights, and stock prices. Membership in any such additional class shall be governed by a separate membership agreement or addendum specific to that class.

## Cross-Cutting Recommendations

**1. Bylaws cross-reference audit.** Before the board authorizes this agreement, confirm the bylaws contain the termination and dispute resolution procedures referenced in Sections 5 and 9.6–9.7. If the bylaws are thin on these points, the protections proposed in Issue 5 should be incorporated directly into the membership agreement.

**2. Tax counsel engagement.** The Patronage Plan (Issue 3) requires a decision between Subchapter T and Subchapter K treatment. This is a foundational choice that affects the Cooperative's tax returns, member notices, and capital account maintenance from the first fiscal year. Jeff's note about the tax lawyer should be followed up before the board adopts the Patronage Plan. The 90-day clock on the Patronage Plan proposed above starts on the date of first patron member admission.

**3. Techne co-op agreement architecture.** When Jeff drafts the $25 Techne membership agreement, it should use consistent definitions for Governing Documents, Board, and Confidential Information. The IP scope for Techne members may differ from Patron Members (Techne members may be doing less Cooperative-Commissioned Work and more general cooperative participation). The two agreements should be read as a system.

**4. Annual patronage notice resolution.** Before the first fiscal year end, the board should adopt a resolution establishing the form and timing of written patronage notices. This is a simple board action but it needs to happen before any patronage is allocated.

**5. "Status of Parties" clause (Section 9.4).** The provision disclaiming agency, partnership, and employment relationships is standard. For a cooperative, it may be worth adding a sentence affirming what the relationship *is*: "Applicant is an owner-member of the Cooperative with the rights and obligations set forth in this Agreement and the Governing Documents."
