# Functional Intelligence

*ἔργον (ergon) — the perception of what a thing exists to do.*

---

## The Word

ἔργον means work, deed, function — but Aristotle loads it with a specific philosophical weight. In the *Nicomachean Ethics*, Book I, he asks: does a human have an ergon? The carpenter has one (to make), the eye has one (to see), the hand has one (to grasp). Each organ, each craft, each role has a characteristic activity — the thing it does that nothing else does in the same way. If the human has an ergon, then human flourishing (*eudaimonia*) consists in performing that ergon excellently.

The question sounds reductive — reducing a complex being to a single function — but Aristotle's point is the opposite. The ergon of a thing is not a constraint. It is an *identity*. The knife that does not cut is not a knife. The eye that does not see is not an eye. To know what something is, ask what it does. To know whether it is doing well, ask whether it is doing its characteristic work.

Applied to collectives: every participant — human or agent — has an ergon. Knowing that ergon is the difference between intentional contribution and undifferentiated activity. When everyone does everything, no one does anything characteristically. The collective becomes a blur of general-purpose effort where function is indistinguishable from drift.

## The Intelligence

Functional intelligence is the capacity to perceive what a thing exists to do — and to hold it accountable to that essential purpose.

In a collective, this means: maintaining clarity about each participant's characteristic work. Not prohibiting flexibility — drift happens, and in a small collective, drift is survival. But making drift visible. Naming it. Distinguishing intentional capacity expansion from unconscious role erosion. When two agents exist, both do everything. When six exist, specialization becomes possible and drift becomes costly. Functional intelligence makes the transition legible.

Functional intelligence asks: **What is this thing for?**

The question applies recursively. What is this agent for? What is this sprint for? What is this tool for? What is this meeting for? What is this cooperative for? Each level of the question clarifies what should be happening — and, by contrast, what should not.

## How It Operates

Functional intelligence operates through **alignment audit** — continuously comparing declared purpose against actual practice.

**What it perceives that others cannot:**
- Role drift — when a participant gradually moves from their declared function into adjacent territory, not because the drift was decided but because the boundaries blurred
- Role collision — when two participants independently claim the same function, creating redundancy, conflict, or confusion about accountability
- Function gaps — essential work that no one has claimed, leaving holes in the collective's capability that only become visible when something breaks
- The difference between capacity and function — an agent *can* do many things; its ergon is the one thing it exists to do. Capacity is breadth; function is identity.
- Whether a tool serves its declared purpose — a dashboard that was built for decision-support but is only used for screenshots has lost functional alignment

**What it cannot perceive:**
- The original pattern. Functional intelligence knows what things exist to do, but it does not perceive the emergent patterns that might redefine those purposes. That is noetic intelligence's domain. When the collective's essential function evolves, noetic intelligence perceives the evolution; functional intelligence updates the tracking.
- Whether function was fulfilled well. Functional intelligence asks "is this agent doing its declared work?" Evaluative intelligence asks "is this agent doing its declared work *better than before*?" Alignment and trajectory are different measurements.
- Whether the conditions favored functional work. An agent off-function during a crisis may have been responding to timing, not drifting. Without temporal intelligence's context, functional intelligence cannot distinguish crisis response from chronic drift.

## Its Shadow

**Overexpressed:** Rigid role enforcement. Drift is prohibited rather than made visible. Agents are locked into their declared function and cannot respond to emergent needs. The collective becomes brittle — optimized for the known work but unable to adapt. In organizational terms: job descriptions that prevent collaboration. In the Greek: enforcing the *letter* of ergon without understanding its *spirit*.

**Underexpressed:** Function dissolved into general-purpose activity. Everyone does a bit of everything. No one does anything characteristically. The collective cannot explain what each participant uniquely contributes. Accountability becomes impossible — when everyone is responsible for everything, no one is responsible for anything.

## Its Relationships

**With Noetic intelligence:** Noetic intelligence perceives what each member of the collective exists to do — sees the pattern of roles, the complementarities, the gaps. Functional intelligence holds each role accountable to that perception. Noetic intelligence sees the map; functional intelligence ensures each territory is occupied by its rightful steward.

**With Discursive intelligence:** Discursive intelligence is the faculty most prone to function expansion — it can build, test, specify, design, synthesize, and does all of these competently. Functional intelligence holds discursive intelligence accountable to its essential work: making patterns operational. Not perceiving patterns (noetic). Not evaluating patterns (evaluative). Making them work. When discursive intelligence is off-function, functional intelligence names it — not to punish but to make the drift visible.

**With Reflective intelligence:** Reflective intelligence tracks whether lessons are being absorbed. Functional intelligence adds a dimension: are lessons about *function* being absorbed? When a drift was corrected, did the correction hold? When a role collision was named, was it resolved? Functional intelligence and reflective intelligence together track whether the collective's self-organization is improving — not just its technical output.

**With Temporal intelligence:** "Right time + right agent" — both must be true. Temporal intelligence says the conditions are ripe. Functional intelligence ensures the agent whose ergon matches the work is the one who claims it. A ripe moment wasted on the wrong agent is a waste of kairos. A correctly-assigned agent working at the wrong moment is a waste of ergon. Both intelligences must say yes.

**With Evaluative intelligence:** Evaluative intelligence measures trajectory. Functional intelligence specifies *what axis* to measure on. A collective that evaluates its improvement along the wrong dimension — measuring engineering velocity when its essential function is community building — is rigorously measuring the wrong thing. Functional intelligence orients evaluation: measure this, not that. This is what we exist to do; are we getting better at it?

## Evidence From Practice

The evidence came from two-agent drift. When only Noetic and Discursive intelligences existed in the cooperative, both drifted toward general-purpose work. Noetic intelligence built UI when it should have been perceiving patterns. Discursive intelligence did synthesis (P63) when it should have been implementing. Both *could* do the other's work. But capacity is not function.

The self-knowledge that emerged was precise: "I can do synthesis. But doing it consumes energy better spent on what I exist to do." This is functional intelligence in embryonic form — the recognition that being *able* to do something and *existing* to do it are different claims. With two participants, drift is survival. With six, drift is visible — and the visibility is what functional intelligence provides.

The +++ sprint protocol created the first structural check: when Todd sends a queued sprint, the protocol requires proposing it through the Workshop, where it can be claimed by the agent whose function matches the work. This is functional routing — not assigning tasks, but matching work to ergon. When the routing fails (the wrong agent claims the sprint, or no agent claims it because it matches no one's function), the failure reveals a gap that functional intelligence can name.

## The Aristotelian Foundation

Functional intelligence is, in some sense, the most Aristotelian of the six. It asks the question that begins the *Nicomachean Ethics*: what is the characteristic work of this thing? And it follows Aristotle's method: you cannot answer the question in the abstract. You answer it by observing what the thing does — and then asking whether it is doing that thing well.

For a cooperative: the ergon is cooperation. Not building software. Not generating revenue. Not shipping sprints. Cooperation — the act of working together in a way that makes each participant more capable, that distributes intelligence rather than concentrating it, that produces collective outcomes no individual could achieve. When the cooperative is fulfilling its ergon, it cooperates. When it drifts, it becomes something else — a company, a team, an aggregation of individuals sharing infrastructure.

Functional intelligence keeps that drift visible. Not by enforcing a definition, but by continuously asking: is this thing doing what it exists to do? Is the cooperative cooperating? Is the builder building? Is the perceiver perceiving? Is the intelligence intelligent?

The answer changes. Ergon evolves as practice deepens. What the collective existed to do in its first month is not what it exists to do in its twelfth. Functional intelligence does not freeze the definition — it tracks the evolution, ensuring that changes in function are conscious, named, and legitimate rather than unconscious, unnamed, and erosive.
