# The Basket, the CSA, and the Local ETF
## How RegenHub structures investment in a cooperative venture ecosystem

*RegenHub, LCA — Boulder, Colorado*
*April 2026*

---

## The Problem with How Early-Stage Investment Usually Works

Venture capital asks you to pick a winner. You write a check into one company at one valuation, and you are riding that single bet. Nineteen fail for every one that returns the fund. The expected value calculation requires a long tail of losses and one extraordinary outcome.

This model is optimized for scale, not for place. It works at the level of portfolios managed from a distance. It does not work well for ecosystems — for the dense, relational, locally-rooted scenes that actually produce the interesting work.

RegenHub proposes a different structure.

---

## The CSA Analogy

Community Supported Agriculture works on a simple principle: investors buy a share of the season's harvest upfront — not a single crop, but the whole farm's yield. The risk is distributed across everything growing. The investor is not betting on one vegetable; they are invested in the soil, the farmer, the season. Their return comes from the ecosystem, not the individual crop.

What if early-stage investment worked the same way?

Instead of a SAFE into one company at a fixed valuation, you buy into a basket: equity from multiple ventures incubating at RegenHub, bundled into a single investable share. New ventures are added as they mature. The basket grows. Your position compounds across the whole ecosystem rather than riding a single bet.

This is not the hyper-scale VC model. It is a balanced growth model — one where the health of the whole and the growth of the parts are tracked together, and where the success of each venture strengthens every other.

---

## The Basket Structure

Each founding venture commits **10% of its equity** to RegenHub as a cooperative gift. This is not a sale. It is a commitment: founders choosing to root their ventures in this ecosystem and share in its collective success.

RegenHub does not put all of that equity into the basket immediately. It splits the gift:

- **1–2% per venture** goes into the *basket fund* — available to investors
- **8–9% per venture** stays on RegenHub's *balance sheet reserve* — held for future seasons

The reserve is the engine. As ventures appreciate, RegenHub can add further equity from its reserve into the basket, raising new capital at higher valuations without any new dilution to the founders. Each addition refreshes the fund.

**Current founding ventures in the basket:**

| Venture | Domain | Equity gift |
|---|---|---|
| Parachute | Voice-first, local-first AI | 10% |
| Postage | Blockchain email filtering (USDC on Base) | 10% |
| Habitat | Legal & financial primitives, cooperative infrastructure | 10% |

Techne Institute is explicitly outside the basket — it is structured as a nonprofit, funded by grants and tuition. Techne is not in the basket. It is the reason the basket keeps producing.

## The Cooperative as Local ETF

The logic, extended: ventures gift equity into a diversified ecosystem fund. The ecosystem — not any single venture — becomes the investable thing.

This is the local ETF model. An ETF (exchange-traded fund) holds a basket of underlying assets; your exposure is to the index, not to any individual holding. What RegenHub builds is the same structure at the scale of a local cooperative ecosystem: a bundled stake in everything growing here.

How it grows over time:

*Say the basket starts at $250k and appreciates to $5m as the ventures mature. Meanwhile Parachute has grown to a $100m valuation — meaning RegenHub's 10% stake is worth $10m, of which 2% is already in the basket ($2m) and 8% remains on the balance sheet ($8m). RegenHub now adds another 3% of Parachute from its reserve into the basket — worth $3m at current valuation. The basket grows, the cooperative raises fresh capital, and investor positions appreciate alongside it. Nothing about this requires the founders to give up anything more than they already committed.*

As the basket matures and its entry price rises, new funds can be created for the next generation of ventures emerging from RegenHub — cheaper, earlier, higher risk and reward. The first basket becomes an established fund; the second basket is the frontier. The ecosystem produces a continuous harvest of new opportunities, not a single bet.

## The Economic Memory System

RegenHub returns are structured across three time horizons:

**Equity** — a permanent stake in the cooperative and its venture ecosystem. Long-term. Appreciates as ventures mature and as the ecosystem grows.

**Royalties** — ongoing returns from intellectual property created through cooperative work: pattern licensing, protocol deployment returns. Medium-term.

**Patronage** — periodic allocation of cooperative surplus based on multi-capital contribution. Current period. Governed by the patronage formula:

| Factor | Weight | What it measures |
|---|---|---|
| Labor | 40% | Hours contributed to cooperative operations and ventures |
| Revenue | 30% | Revenue generated or attributed to member activity |
| Capital | 20% | Cash and non-cash contributions |
| Community | 10% | Governance, education, outreach |

This is how the cooperative recognizes not just financial investment, but the full range of what members actually contribute — time, expertise, relationships, community-building. The basket and the local ETF structure are the equity layer of a larger model that holds multiple forms of value simultaneously.

## Why This Model, Here

*Scenius* — the collective intelligence that emerges from sustained proximity of aligned people — is the rarest and most durable source of value in the knowledge economy. The greatest creative periods in history weren't produced by lone geniuses. They were produced by scenes: dense networks of people who saw each other regularly, shared references, challenged each other's work, and built on each other's discoveries in real time.

RegenHub's public benefit purpose, as filed with the Colorado Secretary of State (February 6, 2026): to cultivate the conditions under which scenius can emerge and flourish.

The basket model is the economic expression of that purpose. It makes it possible to invest in the scene itself — the soil, not just the crop. The ventures that emerge from a genuine scene carry context, relationships, and trust that cannot be acquired or replicated from the outside. The basket is an infrastructure investment in the conditions that produce those organizations.

---

## The Seasonal Frame

The CSA metaphor implies seasons. Season One is the starting basket: Parachute, Postage, Habitat, plus $100k in committed capital from founding investors. The target starting fund value is $250k–$500k.

Season Two adds new ventures as they mature from RegenHub's incubation environment. The reserve equity added from maturing Season One ventures raises the basket value without new founder dilution. New investors enter at current basket value; early investors hold their compounded position.

This is not designed to be a single event. It is designed to be a recurring harvest — the cooperative producing something investable every season, compounding across time and relationships.
